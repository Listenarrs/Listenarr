# Automatic book identity policy

`CorroboratedAutomaticV1` assesses every returned search candidate and passes every
candidate to the existing quality engine. Automatic selection requires a confirmed
identity and a nonrejected, positive quality score. Both search-and-download and
the scheduled worker, including its author-free fallback, use the same service.

A complete supported title with a complete credited author can confirm a work.
Structured provider authors and complete release-name credits are supported;
an inferred `Artist` is not an independent author fact. Display placeholders such
as `Unknown Author` cannot corroborate a title. Only explicitly verified,
candidate-specific identifiers can confirm without author evidence, and reliable
contradictions prevent confirmation.

Exact title-only candidates remain scored and visible for explicit manual choice.
Unverified aliases, surnames, omitted middle initials, arbitrary subtitle prefixes,
bundles, and unexplained extra text defer automatic selection. This is deliberate
and applies equally to short and long titles. Existing profile constraints still
control format, size, language, and other quality preferences.

The interpreter protects a complete reference title before interpreting surrounding
credits or annotations. It does not delete title words or infer identity from token
overlap. A separately stored subtitle supports a main-title form; punctuation alone
does not establish that relationship. Unknown future layouts defer. Provider facts
can still be inaccurate; confirmation is an evidence policy, not proof of audio
contents.

`identity-policy-v1.json` retains 205 controlled scenarios and 210 candidate
observations, including original fixture fields and legacy expectations. Its
`expectedConfirmed` values were explicitly adjudicated under this policy, not
generated by the matcher. Identity confirmation does not override quality rejection.
The feature tests additionally assert statuses, preservation of literal titles,
provenance, identifier assurance, generated layouts, cancellation, source changes,
fresh target/profile validation, quality association, and deterministic selection.

Run the focused policy tests with:

```powershell
dotnet test tests/Listenarr.Tests.csproj -c Release -p:SkipFrontendBuild=true --filter 'FullyQualifiedName~Listenarr.Tests.Features.Application.Search.Matching'
```

No schema migration, title allowlist, new catalogue request, or change to explicit
manual download authorization is required. The existing `SearchResult.Score`
remains the quality score; identity and reasons are separate diagnostics.

The V2 release interpreter keeps source offsets and assigns surrounding text to
author, series, series position, narrator, technical metadata, or group roles.
Scene punctuation is interpreted in a separate comparison view. Complete title
and author evidence is still required. Known series positions must agree with
the library, and unlabeled scene narrators require a matching library narrator.
Unknown annotations and groups defer; they are not arbitrary ignorable text.
Narrator and series membership changes invalidate the assessment fingerprint.
Parsing has input, reference-count, operation, and cancellation limits.

## V4 annotation extensions

The fixed internal rule families separately recognize technical fields, narrators,
groups and reference-backed series. Rules explain raw spans and expose a rule ID,
reference and match strength; they cannot supply missing title or author evidence.
Known numbered series fields are reserved before broader annotation rules so a
technical interpretation cannot hide a position conflict. Unknown library positions
are distinguishable from malformed or contradictory facts. Release-side positions
must also agree with one another, including across both sides of the title.

Series comparison supports punctuation and article presentation plus explicit
aliases scoped to a stored canonical series. Opaque group identifiers remain a
versioned catalog, not a wildcard for arbitrary trailing text. Sizes, supported
scene suffixes and bracketed genres have bounded whole-field rules. Narrator
surnames require a unique stored reference in the supported scene/year/group
layout. A missing reference narrator or unknown series still defers.

### Automatic compatibility (V5)

Work identity remains separate from automatic eligibility. Every result still
receives its normal quality score, and manual selection retains that behavior.
Automatic selection additionally checks language and edition compatibility:

- A recognized scene-language suffix keeps its language meaning and provenance.
  Explicit language metadata that disagrees with the target language or the
  profile's allowed languages defers automatic selection, even with a positive
  score or a verified matching identifier. Conflicting language claims defer too.
- Loose title/description guesses and language copied from request context are
  not promoted into authoritative candidate facts. Missing/unknown language alone
  does not establish a mismatch. Literal title words are not language annotations.
- Explicit edition-sensitive subtitles or title annotations require matching
  edition evidence or a verified identifier for that reference edition. Adaptation
  designations retain their qualifiers; a bare "adapted" tag does not establish
  "Adapted for Modern Readers". Contradictions still defer with a matching ID.
- Abridged/adapted/dramatized releases do not automatically establish compatibility
  with an unspecified target edition. An unabridged label alone does not invent a
  conflict with an otherwise unspecified target. Adaptation and abridgement are
  separate: an unabridged recording of the matching adaptation can be compatible.
- Coauthor completeness is not an edition proxy. Normal collaborations continue
  using the existing credited-author identity policy.

Thus corpus 40 can confirm work identity while deferring under an English profile,
and 86 can confirm the Twain work while deferring for missing adaptation evidence.
Neither the historical `imported` label nor work confirmation proves audio language,
edition compatibility, successful download, or successful import. The frozen gist
files and work-identity adjudications are unchanged; automatic eligibility is tested
separately. Group/alias recall expansions and year-versus-position parsing were
deferred at V5; the reviewed V6 extensions are documented below.

To support another variation, add a sanitized release and reference context,
adjudicate the expected identity independently, extend the existing rule or
catalog, and add a paired wrong-work/conflict case plus raw-span assertions.
Changing semantics requires a policy version bump (and fingerprints for new
reference facts), corpus replay and the affected API/worker checks. Do not derive
trusted aliases or library facts from the candidate release itself.

### Annotation catalog (V6)

The immutable annotation catalog now supplies reviewed group/source labels,
reference-scoped series aliases, bounded series descriptors and narrator-name
data. Its content hash joins assessment fingerprints. The V5 language/edition
compatibility checks remain in force. See [RELEASE_ANNOTATIONS.md](RELEASE_ANNOTATIONS.md)
for placement rules, validation, reviewed aliases and the extension workflow.
`recall-adjudications-v6.json` records independently reviewed new expectations;
the original corpus, fact supplement and adjudications remain frozen.
`prowlarr-recall-pairs.json` preserves 17 real search observations paired with
existing exported target facts, including wrong-book and adaptation controls.
